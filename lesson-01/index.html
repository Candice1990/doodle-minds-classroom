TEACHER CONTROLS · AGES 5–7

One idea: a sequence is an order of steps.

Children arrange three picture commands. The machine executes all three from left to right, including commands that act on an empty table or closed bag. It never repairs the order automatically. The first pumpkin plan deliberately stamps before loading the pumpkin. Run it, observe the result, then swap cards.

Progression: watch and change Pip’s pumpkin plan → build an invitation plan → independently prepare treats → test an alternative lantern order → plan the balloon task → make and share a paper program. The lantern accepts two orders: load, cover, on; or load, on, cover. Explain that changing the order can change the result, but some different orders both work.

Each party job opens with a mission card: read the goal, starting props and machine rules, then invite predictions before opening the activity. The card does not reveal a numbered answer. Use “Read our mission again” to revisit it without changing the picture program. Read stories and picture meanings aloud. Reading English is not required. Use the same refrain: “What is our sequence? First → Next → Last.” Invite children to point and say each step before Run; after a swap, ask what changed. Each machine starts fresh, so no earlier artifact is needed.

Allow about 5 minutes greeting and party plan, 28 minutes on the five machines, 20 minutes partner making/testing, and 7 minutes sharing/closing. Partners swap programmer and robot roles. Support with two cards already placed; extend by testing another order and explaining what changed.

Print the two-page picture-program kit ↗ · A4 landscape · 100% · headers/footers off. Precut cards. Use paper props and pretend actions.

Run freezes the cards and navigation. Stop cancels motion and voice; Reset scene keeps the cards; Clear cards starts a new plan. Slower pace gives more observation time. The original recorded Hello and approved natural cartoon feedback remain local audio assets.
